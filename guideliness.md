# Guidelines for building PMF and selling the maths platform

Agreed paid-launch blueprint, 6 November 2026. First application: **Математически открития**, competitive mathematics for Bulgarian fourth-graders.

**Build one clear offer, help ten new families experience its value, and earn renewals before expanding acquisition.** Parents buy a useful learning outcome; children must find the practice understandable and worth returning to. Both conditions matter.

This document applies Alex Hormozi's `$100M Offers`, `$100M Leads` and `$100M Money Models` in that order. Framework descriptions are based on the author's public companion courses and worksheets, not a full-text review of the books. The offer, prices, examples, targets and experiments below are our application, not results or benchmarks established by those books.

## 2. What to take from the three books

### $100M Offers: choose the customer and make the outcome credible

The author's market checklist considers a meaningful problem, ability to pay, reachable buyers and market growth. His value framework considers the desired result, confidence in achieving it, delay and effort. These are useful questions for designing an offer, not a mathematical prediction of sales. [Market checklist](https://www.acquisition.com/files/pick-your-niche-checklist.pdf), [value worksheet](https://www.acquisition.com/files/pricing-value-checklist.pdf).

For this product, apply those questions as follows:

| Question | Practical decision |
| --- | --- |
| Whose problem? | Start with grade-four families already taking competition preparation seriously |
| What outcome? | The child can apply a method to a fresh problem with less help |
| Why believe it? | Demonstrate explanation, follow-up practice and honest independent/assisted evidence |
| How soon is value visible? | Make the first useful practice session easy to reach; do not promise instant academic improvement |
| What effort can be removed? | Reduce searching for suitable tasks and reconstructing solutions; preserve the child's thinking |

Create the offer by listing parent obstacles, matching each to a concrete product capability, and removing expensive extras that do not address the main problem. Risk reversal should reduce purchase uncertainty. Real capacity can justify a limited intake. Neither requires invented scarcity or exaggerated savings.

### $100M Leads: attract interested buyers consistently

The book's core four are warm outreach, public free content, cold outreach and paid advertising. Its public companion material also covers lead magnets and other people bringing leads through referrals and partnerships. [Official framework material](https://www.acquisition.com/hubfs/%24100M%20Journal.pdf?hsLang=en), [course and resources](https://www.acquisition.com/leads-gifts).

Our application is deliberately narrower: start with introductions and useful content, then add referral requests after value is experienced. A free sample should solve one small problem well enough that a parent can judge the paid experience. Count conversations with suitable parents and resulting purchases, not just impressions or email addresses. Improve the weakest step before adding another channel.

### $100M Money Models: connect acquisition to repeat value and sustainable revenue

The official course distinguishes attraction offers, upsells, downsells and continuity offers, alongside customer-acquisition cost, gross profit and payback. [Offer types](https://www.acquisition.com/training/money/offer-types), [course overview](https://www.acquisition.com/training/money/context).

For launch, the model is **free sample → one monthly subscription → renewal → referral**. Keep one paid plan until additional needs justify more. A future upsell must provide additional service, not remove essential teaching from the base product. A cheaper alternative only helps if its scope and economics work. Recurring billing alone does not create recurring value.

## 3. Positioning: sell useful practice to the parent and deliver it to the child

The first target is a parent who can describe a recent preparation problem: the child follows a worked solution but struggles with a similar task, practice requires constant adult help, or suitable next exercises are difficult to choose. Existing lessons are compatible with the offer; we do not need to replace the teacher.

The long-term aspiration is stronger competition performance. The initial promise is the practice mechanism we can actually provide. Do not claim complete coverage of every admission competition, automatically assessed written proof, live AI tutoring or guaranteed medals/admission.

Use these original Bulgarian copy examples as the starting point for the commercial page:

> **Състезателна математика за 4. клас**
>
> От разбрано решение към самостоятелно решена задача.
>
> Истински състезателни задачи, обяснения стъпка по стъпка и следващо упражнение според напредъка. Детето упражнява метода, а вие виждате какво решава с помощ и самостоятелно.
>
> **Опитайте примерна задача**

Avoid making the product's catalogue size the main argument. A larger library is an alternative parents can already buy. Our differentiation hypothesis is that selecting the next practice and distinguishing assisted from independent success makes learning more useful; validate that hypothesis through behavior and parent feedback.

When speaking with a prospective buyer, ask about the last real occasion the child got stuck, what the family did, what it already uses and pays for, and what would make next month's preparation better. Then show the relevant example and ask whether they want to buy. Hypothetical enthusiasm is weaker evidence than a purchase followed by continued use.

## 4. Package the US$100/month offer

Include access for one child to the available grade-four collections, worked explanations, follow-up practice, saved progress and shared parent/child progress visibility. Present Математика за всеки and Олимпиада as competition preparation; describe НВО as supplementary school-exam practice. Advertise only content actually available and suitable for paid publication.

Include a concise getting-started guide and technical support by the published contact channel. Promise an initial support response within two business days only once the owner has a working mailbox and can sustain that schedule. Technical support does not include marking proofs, solving individual homework or private tuition.

Suggested pricing copy:

> **100 щ.д. на месец за едно дете**
>
> Месечен абонамент без годишно обвързване. Можете да спрете следващото подновяване от профила си. Достъпът остава до края на платения период.
>
> Първото плащане е при записване. Ако платформата не е подходяща за вас, до 14 дни от това плащане можете да прекратите абонамента и да поискате пълно възстановяване на сумата.

Charge US$100 at signup and monthly thereafter. The 14-day window is a first-payment money-back promise, not a free trial or a deferred first charge. Cancellation after that window still stops future renewal and retains access through the paid period; it does not carry the same voluntary first-payment refund promise. Before publication, confirm that US$100 is the final payable consumer price and show any required local price disclosures. Keep USD explicit in the landing page, checkout and receipts; do not silently substitute €100. The voluntary refund promise must not be presented as replacing applicable statutory rights. It has no minimum-practice requirement. Process a qualifying refund through the provider, cancel further renewal and end the refunded paid entitlement after confirmation; keep learner history.

Do not add launch coupons, lifetime discounts, artificial setup fees, three pricing tiers or annual prepayment. These would obscure the first question: will suitable families buy and keep using this product at US$100/month?

## 5. Price and economic checks

### Competitive anchor

During research, the provider's indexed grade 3-4 platform page advertised **€17.90/month**, detailed video solutions and a larger problem collection. The direct page subsequently returned only a minimal JavaScript shell through the research tool, so recheck the live offer before using it in a public comparison. This is one observed alternative, not a comprehensive market survey. [Състезател.БГ platform](https://platform.sastezatel.bg/course/platforma-3-4kl).

US$100/month is the chosen premium-price test, not validated willingness to pay. It must be supported by useful guided practice, understandable progress and child engagement. The competing offer is denominated in EUR, so do not invent a fixed price difference without an exchange-rate date. Test the actual US$100 recurring offer with suitable new parents; purchases and renewals at a discounted introductory price would not validate demand at US$100. Keep the price at US$100 for this test rather than introducing an entry-month discount or subscriber-count price increases.

For the first intake, keep the price and offer stable enough to interpret the result. Do not run a tiny split test and call it statistically decisive. Log purchase objections. If the offer does not convert, first distinguish poor fit, unclear value, trust, payment friction and actual price resistance. Change one major variable for the next intake.

### Cost model

Keep a small spreadsheet with actual values:

| Metric | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Cash collected | Successful charges less refunds; separately identify taxes and fees |
| Recognized net revenue | Revenue for the delivered period, excluding applicable indirect taxes and refunds |
| Monthly contribution | Net revenue less processing, billing, variable hosting and attributable delivery/support costs |
| Cash CAC | Acquisition cash spend divided by new paying families from that effort |
| Founder sales effort | Sales hours per new paying family; report separately from cash CAC |
| Support effort | Support minutes per active family, with an explicit hourly cost assumption |
| CAC payback | Cumulative customer contribution needed to recover acquisition cost |

Ten successful US$100 subscriptions produce **US$1,000 gross monthly charges before taxes, refunds, fees and costs**. That is not US$1,000 profit. Keep the subscription revenue in USD and the operating budget in EUR explicit; convert for accounting using the applicable recorded rate rather than treating the currencies as equal. Do not estimate lifetime value by dividing revenue by a churn percentage from ten families; track observed cumulative contribution instead.

Treat €100/month as a spending ceiling, not a target. Inventory the existing hosting/tool costs first and check that the hosting plan permits the commercial use. Start with no paid ads, no CMS and no additional subscription analytics service. If required operating costs exceed the ceiling, report the gap before purchasing anything.

**Default: Stripe Checkout + Billing + Customer Portal**, subject to approval for the existing business. **Alternative: Mollie**, if account eligibility, applicable fees or operational experience make it preferable. Do not integrate two providers for launch or reactivate the frozen myPOS setup as a side task.

| Mollie, domestic consumer card | 1.2% + €0.25 |

These published EUR-denominated rate bases are not a fee quote for a US$100 charge. Confirm USD presentment, settlement currency, conversion, recurring-payment support and account-specific charges before selecting the live configuration. Recalculate provider costs in one currency after those inputs are known. Card categories, tax services, disputes and refunds can also change the economics. Compare the confirmed costs for ten US$100 monthly charges alongside implementation and support time; the earlier euro fee estimates do not apply to this USD offer. Mollie also supports subscriptions; confirm the customer-management workflow before switching the implementation direction. [Stripe portal](https://docs.stripe.com/customer-management), [Mollie subscriptions](https://docs.mollie.com/reference/create-subscription).

Provider onboarding, business verification, bank details and configuration remain owner-account dependencies. Company formation is outside scope. Before live billing, the existing business's accountant must confirm treatment of receipts/invoices, taxes, refunds and reporting; a processor-generated invoice is not by itself proof that all local accounting obligations are satisfied.

## 7. Brand and landing-page brief

Keep the working name **Математически открития** and existing visual identity. Use the cream background, dark teal primary actions, warm white surfaces, blue learning progress and gold rewards already defined in the design system. Reuse the existing typography, components and illustrations; put any final presentation adjustments in that guide rather than create competing tokens here.

The parent-facing presentation should feel calm, specific and credible. Let real product screens explain the experience. The otter and islands support the child-facing world without turning the sales page into a game advertisement. Use short Bulgarian sentences and concrete benefits. Do not invent badges, testimonials, expert qualifications or learning outcomes.

Build the page in this order:

| Section | Content and purpose |
| --- | --- |
| First screen | Grade-four audience, independent-solving benefit, sample CTA and visible existing-user login |
| How practice works | A real example of task → help → related practice; show what changes after an error |
| What parents can see | Actual independent/assisted progress, with an anonymized or synthetic demonstration |
| Included and suitable | Available collections, self-paced format and limitations; no tutoring promise |
| Price | US$100/month, one child, renewal, cancellation and refund promise, plus purchase CTA |
| Questions | Suitable level, parent involvement, Google login, saved work, cancellation and support |
| Footer | Existing business identity/contact, commercial terms, privacy and useful resources |

The primary action before purchase intent is **„Опитайте примерна задача“**. The price section's action is **„Започнете за 100 щ.д. на месец“** and leads to authenticated checkout. Existing families must retain an easy route to login.

## 8. Free sample and first paid experience

Use one coherent demonstration: an initial reviewed competition task, optional worked help and a fresh related application. Let the child try before revealing the solution. Explain the method using the actual numbers, then show why the next task is related. Do not label completion as a diagnosis of ability or competition readiness.

The sample must work without an account or card. Keep its state local to the demonstration and clearly disclose that progress is not saved. It must not create learner records, award gold or write to existing learning history. Sample completion means completing the walkthrough through its closing explanation, regardless of correctness; a child may still stop earlier.

End with a short parent-facing explanation of what the subscription adds and a purchase action. Do not place a paywall halfway through the promised sample solution.

After purchase, guide the parent through one child profile and the first suitable adventure. Offer the existing optional child-login step without making it a purchase obstacle. The child should reach useful practice without a long questionnaire. The first-week parent guide should explain starting, pausing, hints and reading progress; it is not an extra coaching service.

## 9. Billing and access behavior for the implementation backlog

Use the current Google/Supabase parent identity. The parent owns billing; the paid entitlement belongs to one child. Children never manage payments. An eligible new parent must be able to authenticate and purchase without first joining the pilot invitation list; authentication alone must not grant paid practice access.

Implementation must add server-side customer/subscription references, a paid entitlement and durable webhook processing. Keep account mapping in the database rather than trusting an email or browser redirect as proof of ownership. Use the provider's current subscription documentation when implementing. [Stripe lifecycle](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/overview).

| Situation | Required behavior |
| --- | --- |
| First charge succeeds | Grant one-child paid access only after verified server-side confirmation; complete child setup if needed |
| Checkout abandoned or first charge fails | No new paid entitlement; offer a recoverable return path |
| Duplicate click or repeated webhook | No duplicate active subscription, charge request or entitlement |
| Redirect returns before webhook | Show a pending state and recheck; do not grant access from the success URL |
| Renewal succeeds | Extend paid access to the confirmed period |
| Renewal fails | Notify the parent through billing communications and allow seven days from the first failed renewal attempt to resolve payment |
| Grace period expires | Suspend paid practice without deleting history; keep billing/recovery accessible |
| Parent cancels | Stop future renewal; retain access to the end of the paid period |
| Qualifying first-payment refund | Refund and stop future renewal; revoke the refunded entitlement after confirmation |
| Parent returns later | Reuse the existing child and history; do not create another learner automatically |
| Provider events arrive late/out of order | Reconcile against current provider state so old events cannot restore expired access |

Keep subscription state and learner history separate. Apply family authorization and RLS to commercial records. Provide a parent-only customer-portal entry point for payment methods, invoices and cancellation. Keep provider secrets and card data out of the client and application logs.

The existing five-child global pilot cap cannot serve as the commercial access rule. The paid release needs a separate ten-new-family intake limit while preserving existing pilot entitlements. Reserve checkout capacity atomically with expiry and reconcile it on completion so concurrent buyers are not charged for unavailable places. Do not repurpose or erase pilot profiles to make room. Detailed schema and migrations belong to that implementation slice and must be tested on disposable data before release.

Use Search Console data when available and parent questions when it is not; do not invent search volumes or expect SEO to deliver ten families by a particular date. ## 11. Reach and sell to the first ten families

Use one acquisition log with parent contact, source, stated need, next step, outcome and objection. Keep it private. Do not put children’s answers, school details or raw learning history into a sales spreadsheet.

Within three to five hours weekly, allocate approximately two hours to relevant outreach/follow-up, one hour to onboarding/support and up to two hours to content and resolving the main funnel obstacle. If support consumes the available time, pause intake rather than quietly add coaching to the offer.

Start with genuine introductions and communities where an administrator permits the contribution. Approach tutors as a complement to lessons. The founder sends messages; this blueprint does not authorize automated outreach, scraped parent lists or messages to children.

Original introduction example:

> Здравейте, правя платформа за състезателна математика за 4. клас. Детето получава обяснение при затруднение и после упражнява метода върху друга задача. Ето безплатен пример без регистрация: [връзка]. Ако търсите такава подготовка, мога да отговоря на въпросите ви за месечния достъп.

Original tutor introduction:

> Разработвам самостоятелна практика между уроците за четвъртокласници. Бихте ли разгледали този пример и преценили дали е подходящ за някои от вашите ученици? Платформата не включва лични уроци и не оценява автоматично писмени доказателства.

For an interested parent, use a short conversation:

1. Ask what the child is preparing for and where practice currently breaks down.
2. Show a matching example and explain the boundaries.
3. State US$100/month, included access, cancellation and refund terms directly.
4. Ask whether they want the purchase link; record the actual answer.
5. Follow up once after a few days if appropriate, then stop unless they invite continued contact.

Treat objections as information. For “we already have a tutor,” explain complementary practice. For “my child will not use it,” suggest trying the sample together rather than promising motivation. For “too expensive,” ask what they compare it with; do not immediately discount. For “will this get us into СМГ?”, explain the practice benefit without promising admission.

Request a referral only after the family reports useful value. Ask for an introduction, not a positive review as a condition of access or refund. Launch without an affiliate or referral-discount system.

## 12. Measure PMF through learning value and repeat purchase

The buyer and learner have different jobs. A parent paying while the child rarely practises is not a healthy result. A child enjoying a free sample without the parent buying is not commercial validation.

Use these minimum definitions:

| Signal | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Qualified conversation | A parent of a fourth-grader expresses a concrete relevant preparation need |
| Sample completion | Walkthrough reaches its final explanation; do not equate this with learning |
| Paid family | First payment succeeds; retain refunded families in the original cohort and report refunds separately |
| Activation | Within seven days of payment, the child completes an adventure with at least one submitted whole-task answer; revealed-only browsing does not count |
| Active learning week | At least one completed whole-task attempt in that week, including an incorrect attempt; a homepage visit does not count |
| First renewal | Second monthly payment succeeds, including an explicitly reported recovery during the grace window |
| Learning evidence | Fresh independent solving and later recall, separate from hints, repeats, rewards and app visits |

Measure the first four weeks relative to each family's payment date, using Europe/Sofia for displayed dates. Do not compare an early family’s month with a newly enrolled child’s first day. Keep counts and denominators visible.

After all ten families have reached the relevant observation window, review these **provisional management gates**:

| Gate | Initial target | If it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Activation | At least 8 of 10 within seven days | Observe onboarding and the first task; fix access, clarity or level mismatch |
| Continued use | At least 7 of 10 practise in three of their first four weeks | Ask about relevance, difficulty, schedule and willingness to return |
| First renewal | At least 7 of 10 renew | Separate value/price objections, content exhaustion, seasonality and payment failures |
| Learning value | Review fresh independent outcomes for each family with enough comparable evidence; explicitly label insufficient evidence | Inspect task comparability, hinted dependence and suitability before claiming improvement |
| Founder capacity | Sales and support stay within five hours weekly | Simplify the process or slow intake |

These are not Hormozi benchmarks, scientific effect estimates or proof of PMF. Do not change grading, difficulty or reward rules to hit them. Current app progress can include repeated successes, so it cannot be presented wholesale as new independent learning.

Review learning examples internally with authorization and compare fresh tasks using related skills and comparable difficulty. No fresh problem means insufficient evidence, not automatic mastery. A three-problem routing diagnosis is not a competition-readiness assessment. Actual competition outcomes, when voluntarily shared, are longer-term context rather than causal proof that the platform produced the result.

At renewal, ask: What was useful? Where did the child still need help? Would you pay for another month at this price? What would you use instead? Record actual payment beside the answers. For cancellation, allow an optional reason without obstructing cancellation.

Keep adult funnel measurement separate from private educational records. Use existing authorized learning evidence and manual review first; add only the minimum cohort reporting needed in the paid release. Do not send child identities, answers, task IDs or history to advertising tools.

## 13. Renewal, expansion and later monetization

Inspect the first and second renewals before materially increasing acquisition. If the gates hold, admit another small intake based on actual support capacity, then check whether the result repeats beyond personal introductions. Do not silently open unlimited registration after ten purchases.

For recurring value, check whether children have suitable unseen work, understand the help, and return to related practice. The catalogue is finite. Monitor exhaustion and plan reviewed additions only where needed; do not advertise unlimited new material or every-week content drops without delivery capacity.

Distinguish voluntary cancellation, payment failure, competition-season completion and grade progression. Seasonal completion can be a successful use case even when it lowers retention. Grade-four results do not establish demand for another grade.

| Later option | Evidence needed before developing it |
| --- | --- |
| Annual subscription | Sustained value across months and transparent seasonal suitability; not a way to conceal churn |
| Sibling access | Repeated requests plus separate child entitlements and appropriate content |
| Extra grade/competition | Demonstrated existing value, confirmed demand and reviewed new content |
| Paid human review | Clear demand, qualified delivery capacity and economics; a separate service from launch |
| Lower-priced plan | A distinct smaller scope that remains useful, without stripping necessary explanations |
| Paid ads | Working organic purchase flow, acceptable retention and a defined cash-loss limit |

Change one major offer/channel variable at a time. Record the hypothesis, audience, change, observation window and decision. Review actual contribution as well as conversion; collecting more cash is not enough if support costs or refunds grow faster.
